# RouteMate AI — Ethics and Privacy

## Risk profile
Location and mobility traces can reveal homes, workplaces, routines, health visits, and social relationships. Matching can create safety, discrimination, accessibility, and unequal-burden risks. A route recommendation is consequential even when presented as convenience.

## Principles and controls
- **Purpose limitation:** collect only fields necessary for the approved research question.
- **Data minimization:** prefer coarse zones, time windows, and ephemeral IDs over addresses and persistent identifiers.
- **Consent and withdrawal:** provide plain-language purpose, retention, sharing, risks, and withdrawal/deletion path for real participants.
- **Access:** role-based least privilege, audit logs, encryption, secret management, and separated identifiers.
- **Retention:** define deletion dates per dataset and securely delete derived data that can re-identify people where feasible.
- **Fairness:** test disparate burden; do not infer protected traits from routes; include accessibility constraints and appeal/override paths.
- **Safety:** do not expose exact pickup locations by default; provide reporting, cancellation, and emergency guidance appropriate to the study.

## Research governance
Synthetic data needs no participant consent but must be labeled. Simulated demos must not solicit real users under a false impression of operational service. Real-data or human-subject work requires institutional/organizational review, data agreements, and a documented incident process before collection.

## Transparency
Disclose routing source, uncertainty, objective trade-offs, limitations, and evidence class. Never describe synthetic or simulated outputs as observed outcomes, and never claim safety or emissions benefits without validated measurement.
